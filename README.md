# Christopher Woodyard

**I make strange things and test whether they work.**

Researcher · educator · musician · artist · founder of [Vers3Dynamics](https://vers3dynamics.com/), an open-source resonant intelligence lab in the Washington, DC area.

A laboratory inside a simulated city. An AI at the wheel of a midnight coffee run. A map where you can redraw a neighborhood and send the same storm through it again. A sensor that has to admit when it cannot know.

I build these things to study a question that keeps changing shape: **what gives an intelligent system grounds to believe something—or to act on it?** The work brings together experimental worlds, biosignals, statistical measurement, and human authority. My music, paintings, and poetry belong to the same practice: making something difficult to notice available to experience.

### 03 / Quantum Noise Profiler · Can a warning earn your trust?

*On the loop: observe → measure → preserve*

[Quantum Noise Profiler](https://github.com/topherchris420/quantum-noise-profiler) studies qubit-noise monitoring: how to notice meaningful deterioration without confusing a changed workload with changed hardware, and when to withhold an estimate.

It distinguishes vendor observations, workload-inferred values, and dedicated probe measurements. Its monitoring methods track a session error budget; its fits and forecasts can refuse unsupported answers. The work combines established sequential statistics with explicit measurement provenance and model checks.

The first design produced frequent false alarms. Later preregistered simulations tested repairs, including fixed readout probes and drift-aware measurement sweeps. **Conventional CUSUM remained equally effective in the operational comparison.** No quantum-hardware experiment has run, and calibrated empirical thresholds must not be confused with anytime-valid guarantees under their declared assumptions.

[**Inspect the profiler →**](https://github.com/topherchris420/quantum-noise-profiler) · [**Read the follow-up results →**](https://github.com/topherchris420/quantum-noise-profiler/blob/d888fa421b1a843608838935976730a6aea093ad/results/V03_SUMMARY.md) · [**Check the statistical assumptions →**](https://github.com/topherchris420/quantum-noise-profiler/blob/d888fa421b1a843608838935976730a6aea093ad/docs/SEQUENTIAL_METHODS.md)

### 04 / Mannahatta · Send the same storm through a different city

*On the loop: observe → propose → measure → replay*

[Mannahatta](https://github.com/topherchris420/cognisync-terrain-weaver) connects three frames: historical ecological reconstruction, present land cover, and a possible redesign. Draw trees or bioswales into a study area, hold the storm and terrain fixed, and compare where the modeled water goes.

The useful artifact is the **controlled counterfactual you can export and replay**: the question, assumptions, drawings, terrain, substrate identity, and routed outcomes travel together. Reopening a study checks its identities and reruns the comparison.

It is an exploratory planning instrument. Its holdout comparison with reported street flooding **did not beat simple low-elevation ranking**. Classification, coefficients, terrain resolution, and routing limitations remain visible in the [experiment index](https://github.com/topherchris420/cognisync-terrain-weaver/blob/d797b189f7e2d8df9fa75df26e77246e52da0210/experiments/INDEX.md).

### 05 / James + Pordenone · Give intelligence an explicit boundary

*On the loop: validate → authorize → act → replay*

**[James](https://github.com/topherchris420/james)** brings the question to your desktop. Chat through a model app on your machine, show it your screen when you choose, and review one exact click or keystroke before Windows input is dispatched. James waits for you to check the result. It reads R.A.I.N. Lab's records but cannot steer the lab.

Its Rust client can address only a loopback port. Your model app determines whether inference is local; James warns when the app reports a cloud model. Builds exist for Windows, macOS, and Linux, but this remains a **developer preview**: real-device installation, reviewed input, accessibility, and model support have important gaps.

### 06 / TRIAD · Study the answers a system should refuse

*On the loop: measure → replay*

[TRIAD](https://github.com/topherchris420/triad) began with sparse capacitive localization and widened into selective prediction, real-data monitoring, and claim-relative evidence assessment. Its Rust evaluator measures errors among accepted answers; frozen studies ask whether added warning and evidence rules improve decisions.

A converged optimizer could still return a badly wrong position. Later studies found that the proposed gates did not establish an advantage over strong comparators. In the strongest claim-review test, the evidence rules lost to a blind statistical reviewer and cost more than endorsing nothing.

**The negative findings are the research.** The reusable evaluator implements established selective-risk metrics; those metrics are not claimed as an invention. Headline claims are linked to archived fields and rechecked in CI.

[**Use the evaluator →**](https://github.com/topherchris420/triad#start-with-the-evidence) · [**Follow the research arc →**](https://github.com/topherchris420/triad#the-research-arc-machine-checked) · [**Inspect Milestone 8 →**](https://github.com/topherchris420/triad/blob/7be64c8c93f2ef3238ac18b386aa5bb81c214bbb/docs/metrology/milestone8-results.md)

The blind comparators were written by an AI agent of the same model family, from a brief alone. There has been no human adjudication, unrelated-team replication, or peer review. Sensor studies remain synthetic and failed numerical readiness gates.

## The idea

An answer is the beginning of an investigation. I want to see what was observed, which explanation was proposed, what the system was allowed to do, and what actually happened.

One loop runs through the lab:

**observe → propose → validate → authorize → act → measure → preserve → replay**

The authorize step belongs to a person, or to a deterministic policy a person wrote. A model's inference is not evidence, its confidence is not permission, and a beautiful simulation is not a measurement of the real world.

## How I work

**Maximum conceptual ambition; minimum credible experiment.** Build something, decide what would falsify it, keep what happened, and build again.

The 72-hour question: *if a meaningful version had to exist within 72 hours, what would we actually need to build?* It forces a large idea into an experiment small enough to examine. AI can shorten implementation. It cannot manufacture independent evidence. [What the Git timelines show →](experiments/unreasonably-fast.md)
